Explanatory claims (no figures, standard economics, keep the wording general):
- The island example is a simplified model (quantity theory of money). Label it "Simplified example" on screen.
- Zimbabwe printed money to finance government spending while output fell. Hanke and Kwok attribute the hyperinflation to money creation by the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe.
- Countries that borrow in foreign currency face bigger debts when their currency falls.
- Central bank independence is designed to keep money creation away from short-term political pressure.

Accuracy rules for the build:
- Never claim US prices rose 40%. The 40% is the money supply, not prices.
- Show "79.6 billion percent" on screen (the exact figure), even though the voice says "seventy-nine billion".
- Show "24.7 hours" on screen for the doubling time.

### Scene 01: Hook
Starts at: "This is a real banknote."
Ends after: "make everyone rich?"

- Scene opens on a dark navy paper surface. A single cream paper banknote slides into the center, lands with a soft tap and a small wobble. Generic design: simple border, "100,000,000,000,000" in cut paper digits.
- **On "One hundred trillion dollars":** the zeros pop up one by one on twos, each with a tiny lift.
- **On "January two thousand nine":** a small paper calendar tag pins to its corner: "Jan 2009".
- **On "about thirty US dollars":** a small amber price tag swings in on a paper string: "≈ $30".
- **On "doubling roughly every twenty-five hours":** a paper clock appears beside the note. Its hand spins once around, and a paper price tag above a loaf of bread flips from "$1" to "$2", then spins again and flips to "$4". Small flat label: "Prices doubled every 24.7 hours".
- **On "why doesn't every country just print as much as it wants":** a simple paper printing press slides in. Banknotes start popping out of it.
- **On "make everyone rich":** the notes pile up into a tall stack that wobbles. Hold.

### Scene 03: The island

- **On "each loaf costs one dollar":** each loaf gets a small tag "$1".

### Scene 04: Money isn't wealth
Starts at: "Money isn't wealth."
Ends after: "doesn't create more bread."

## Description draft

Why can't a government just print money to make everyone rich? We break it down with a tiny island economy, Zimbabwe's 100 trillion dollar note, and the US money supply growing 40% in two years, and explain why the US avoided hyperinflation.
