## Before posting anywhere

- **Length:** YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels both take clips up to 3 minutes, so all four fit. **Facebook:** if the Reels composer refuses a clip over 90 seconds, post it from your Page as a normal video instead (it still plays vertical in the feed); or post only Short 1 as a Reel.
- **Captions are burned in:** turn **off** each platform's auto-captions so the words don't show twice.
- **Order:** publish the full video on YouTube first, so every Short can point to it.
- Music: the same Envato track as the main video, covered by the channel registration.

## YouTube Shorts

Settings for every Short:
- **Related video:** the main video. This puts a tappable link under the Short.
- Not made for kids, Category Education, language English. No caption file. Altered content: No.
- Leave "Allow remixing" on.

### Short 1: you can't print bread (1:31)

Title:
```
Why Not Just Print More Money? #shorts
```
Description:
```
Imagine an island with 10 loaves of bread and 10 dollars: each loaf costs $1. The government prints 10 more dollars. Everyone has twice the money, but there are still only 10 loaves, so the price moves toward $2. Nobody got richer. Money isn't wealth: bread is. You can print money. You can't print bread.
(The island is a simplified example.)

Full explanation: the related video linked above.
Education only, not financial advice.

#money #inflation #economics
```

### Short 2: the $100 trillion note (1:37)

Title:
```
This $100 Trillion Note Was Worth $30 #shorts
```
Description:
```
Zimbabwe issued a 100 trillion dollar note in January 2009. On its first day it was worth about 30 US dollars. At the peak, prices rose 79.6 billion percent a month and doubled every 24.7 hours. Who decides how much money gets printed, and why is it kept away from politicians?
Sources: BBC News (2009); Hanke and Kwok, Cato Journal (2009).

Full explanation: the related video linked above.
Education only, not financial advice.

#money #zimbabwe #economics
```

### Short 3: the US printed too (1:56)

Title:
```
The US Printed Trillions. Why No Hyperinflation? #shorts
```
Description:
```
US money (M2) went from $15.5 trillion in February 2020 to $21.8 trillion in March 2022: up 40.6%. Inflation came (9.1% in June 2022, the highest in 40 years), but not hyperinflation. Four reasons: timing, the whole world wants dollars (57% of reserves), the US borrows in its own currency, and trust in an independent central bank.
Sources: Federal Reserve H.6 via FRED; US Bureau of Labor Statistics; IMF COFER (2026 Q1).

Full explanation: the related video linked above.
Education only, not financial advice.

#money #inflation #economics
```

### Short 4: how fast is too fast (1:41)

Title:
```
Zimbabwe vs the US: How Fast Is Too Fast? #shorts
```
Description:
```
Zimbabwe printed money to pay its bills while the economy shrank: 79.6 billion percent inflation a month. The US printed too, and got 9.1%. The difference is speed. Central banks aim for about 2% inflation a year, and use higher interest rates as the brake when money grows faster than the economy.
Sources: Hanke and Kwok (2009); Federal Reserve H.6 via FRED; US Bureau of Labor Statistics; Federal Reserve.

Full explanation: the related video linked above.
Education only, not financial advice.

#money #inflation #economics
```
